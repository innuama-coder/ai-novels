# Research Sources

Accessed 2026-08-06. These sources inform method; frozen HLD contracts control implementation.

1. [The Purpose of Scenes — Writer's Digest](https://www.writersdigest.com/wd-books/showing-and-telling-excerpt) — lists scene functions and distinguishes causal plot from mere action; used for the obligation/function register.
2. [Four Questions to Ask When Writing Scenes — Writer's Digest](https://www.writersdigest.com/improve-my-writing/4-questions-to-ask-yourself-when-writing-scenes) — connects intention, opposition, plot relevance, and consequence; used for function preconditions and resistance.
3. [The Fundamentals of Writing a Scene — Jane Friedman](https://janefriedman.com/writing-scene/) — treats scenes as purposeful miniatures with consequence and forward demand; used for exit-state and carry-forward criteria.
4. [First Aid for Scenes — Writer's Digest](https://www.writersdigest.com/improve-my-writing/first-aid-for-scenes) — recommends multi-duty scenes while judging contribution to the whole; used for compatible secondary functions and removal tests.
5. [What Does a Scene Do? — Writer's Digest](https://www.writersdigest.com/improve-my-writing/what-does-a-scene-do) — classifies action, reaction, setup, and intensity roles; used for function taxonomy without fixed templates.
6. [Writing Scenes: Stepping Forward, Falling Back — Writer's Digest](https://www.writersdigest.com/improve-my-writing/writing-scenes-stepping-forward-falling-back) — requires scene goals to affect story direction; used to reject purposeless middle functions.
7. [A New Way to Think About Scene Structure — Helping Writers Become Authors](https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/scene-structure/) — links goal, conflict, outcome and causal continuity; used for precondition/trigger/change contracts.
8. [Scene Structure and Transitions in Big Scenes — Helping Writers Become Authors](https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/scene-structure-and-transitions-in-big-scenes/) — shows how outcome, reaction, dilemma, and decision connect units; used for LWR entry/exit grouping.
9. [Value Shift 101 — Story Grid](https://storygrid.com/value-shift-101/) — requires a meaningful value shift and identifies turning-point consequences; used for material state-change tests.
10. [The Five Commandments of Storytelling — Story Grid](https://storygrid.com/five-commandments-of-storytelling/) — provides incident/complication/crisis/climax/resolution as a diagnostic; used as an optional stress test, not a mandatory template.
11. [How to Write the Perfect Scene — MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-write-the-perfect-scene) — emphasizes specific purpose, new information, POV, change, and transition; used for cross-domain bindings.
12. [Scenes — David Mamet MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/classes/david-mamet-teaches-dramatic-writing/chapters/scenes) — emphasizes goal pursuit and removing unnecessary scenes; used for necessity and anti-filler checks.
