# HC1 methodology

## Method

Treat an arc as the causal path by which pressure exposes a character's current
orientation, choice commits them, cost makes the choice meaningful, and
consequence establishes a new state. Do not treat an arc as a label such as
“becomes braver.”

1. **Freeze authorized personhood** — record the existing goal, belief/value,
   responsibility, relationship, capability, permission, information, and
   authorized end condition. Missing biography is outside HC1.
2. **Participation map** — for each line, identify what the character must
   pursue, oppose, witness, enable, protect, decide, suffer, or legitimately
   miss. A role must affect line progress or meaning.
3. **State dimensions** — track only dimensions supported by authority:
   goal/strategy, knowledge/interpretation, value/worldview, responsibility,
   and relationship state. A change needs a before, trigger, choice, cost, and
   after.
4. **Placement alternatives** — compare at least two legal placements for
   pressure, commitment, or consequence. Evaluate event causality, line
   function, relationship reciprocity, agency, and downstream information
   needs.
5. **Choice-cost-consequence chain** — the character must choose among live
   alternatives; cost must remove or damage something they value; consequence
   must alter later options, relationships, obligations, or outcomes.
6. **Flat and negative arcs** — a steadfast character still changes the world
   or other people under pressure. A negative arc records chosen deterioration
   and its consequences without moralizing.
7. **Action feasibility** — separately test capability, actual knowledge,
   permission/access, and available opportunity. Desire alone does not confer
   ability or information.
8. **Chapter agency** — for every HA1 chapter shell, state separately who may
   carry the reader's viewpoint, which named person or source-bound institution
   may make a consequential choice, who may bear its cost, and who is
   unavailable. Give every legal focal character one
   exact information-acquisition path. Unconscious, absent, or memory-only
   characters may remain affected subjects or cost bearers, but cannot perceive
   later events or make decisions for rescuers, police, institutions, or other
   people. When an institution acts, record its concrete responsibility and
   authority limit rather than borrowing a convenient character identity.
9. **Responsibility/state separation** — the participation register answers in
   natural Chinese what each role is responsible for. Every item begins with
   `负责`, contains no unexplained
   English prose and never narrates whether somebody died, survived, lost
   consciousness, recovered, or returned. Single-letter story names such as C,
   M, and R-07 remain legal. Put changing lifecycle facts in the ordered
   chapter-agency contracts so current HA1/HS1 continuity, not stale baseline
   wording, controls downstream design.
   Treat the deterministic syntax and lexical checks as an early guard, not a
   substitute for fresh semantic review of euphemisms and authority conflicts.
10. **In-world duty** — write what the person or institution must do, protect,
    decide, verify, prevent, or deliver inside the story world. Do not turn a
    narrative function into a responsibility: nobody is responsible for moving
    the plot, creating suspense, maintaining a failure path, or paying off a
    setup. Translate such notes into the actor's concrete goal and authority.
11. **Return coverage** — when HC1 is resolving a sealed return, treat its
    affected participation IDs as an exact ordered set. Preserve every stable
    ID and its character identity; a shorter “main cast” list is not a valid
    substitute for complete requested coverage.

## Semantic anchors

### Pass

- Core changes are event-driven, continuous, and paid for.
- Choices express character rather than merely obeying plot necessity.
- Relationship changes record both sides and observable consequences.
- Every key result has an authorized responsible actor.
- HI1 can derive knowledge-holder obligations without inventing psychology.

### Revise

Character authority is sufficient, but pressure, choice, cost, relationship
step, or participation placement needs HC1-local correction.

### Fail

The arc works only by adding or changing a person, feeling, ability, knowledge,
relationship, motivation, or event fact; or an author-owned question is needed.

## Counterexamples

- A “turning point” that changes belief without a trigger, choice, or cost.
- A result with no character responsible for producing or accepting it.
- One-sided relationship change while the other party's state is unspecified.
- Granting information or access because the plot needs the action.
- Filling out appearance, childhood, or social-circle details as arc work.
