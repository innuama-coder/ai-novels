# HI1 Semantic Rubric

## Verdicts

- `pass`: all applicable anchors pass with story-object evidence and no actionable finding.
- `revise`: only HI1's POV, information timing, chain structure, or evidence package needs local correction.
- `fail`: an upstream fact, arc, event, narrative contract, author answer, or reality dependency must change. Record defect origin and affected IDs; do not route it yourself.

## Pass anchors

1. **POV legality:** each assignment has a valid perceiver and cannot expose prohibited information.
2. **Knowledge provenance:** every knowledge-state transition names a pre-transition state, source/access path, and post-transition state.
3. **Reader contract:** reader-ahead/behind relations are intentional and traceable; no narrator cheat is required.
4. **Suspense closure:** mandatory setups, probes, reveals, and payoffs are complete or explicitly authorized open.
5. **Inference fairness:** misbeliefs and reversals are supported by available evidence and survive retrospective review.
6. **Authority preservation:** source classifications, open flags, authority classes, and resolutions are unchanged.
7. **Boundary:** no dialogue, clue prose, exact memory content, action choreography, or new story fact appears.
8. **Parent consumption:** the result envelope permits one deterministic next action and points only to current sealed evidence.

## Blockers

POV leakage; knowledge without source; reveal without setup; mandatory setup without payoff; an open answer silently chosen; `required_now` followed by a candidate; deliberate character amnesia; unregistered source read; source/HLD ownership conflation; LLD or prose leakage; content package/result envelope/completion conflation.

## Counterexample anchor

A limited POV protagonist already knows the attack plan, but the architecture hides that knowledge from the reader without an authorized unreliable-narrator rule. This is not suspense; it is an epistemic contradiction and must not pass.
