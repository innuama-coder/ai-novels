# Research Sources

Accessed 2026-08-06. These sources inform method only; the frozen HLD catalog remains the contract authority.

1. [Focalisation — University of Tübingen](https://uni-tuebingen.de/en/233930) — separates “who speaks” from “who perceives”; used for narrator/focalizer split and epistemic permission.
2. [Focalization — Living Handbook of Narratology](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/lhn/node/18.html) — defines focalization as restriction of narrative information; used for POV access rules.
3. [A Note on Suspense — Open University](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/creative-writing/start-writing-fiction/content-section-3.4) — frames suspense as managed expectations and desire to know what follows; used for question-state design.
4. [Perspective Taking and Creative Writing — Open University](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/what-happens-you-when-you-read/content-section-5.2) — connects viewpoint with reader identification; used to assess emotional cost of POV choice.
5. [Genre and Reader Expectations — Open University](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/creative-writing/start-writing-fiction/content-section-3.3/?printable=1) — treats genre as a reader contract involving fulfilment and divergence; used for reveal/payoff honesty.
6. [Dramatic Irony — TED-Ed](https://ed.ted.com/lessons/in-on-a-secret-that-s-dramatic-irony-christopher-warner) — defines the audience-knows/character-does-not relation; used for reader/character knowledge matrices.
7. [What Killed the Cat? Curiosity, Suspense, and Surprise](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.TIME.2024.10) — formalizes awareness and three narrative-tension states; used to distinguish prospective suspense, diagnostic curiosity, and surprise.
8. [Inducing Narrative Tension Through Suspense, Surprise, and Curiosity](https://uvadoc.uva.es/bitstream/handle/10324/52500/Inducing-narrative-tension.pdf?sequence=1) — models tension as information imbalance; used for explicit reader-state transitions.
9. [Dark Secrets and Dastardly Lies — Writer's Digest](https://www.writersdigest.com/write-better-fiction/dark-secrets-and-dastardly-lies-ramping-up-a-killer-plot) — emphasizes fair clues, red herrings, secrets, and lies; used for retrospective-fairness checks.
10. [Point of View — Open Oregon Pressbooks](https://openoregon.pressbooks.pub/wrd/chapter/point-of-view/) — describes intimacy/information trade-offs among viewpoints; used in POV option comparison.
11. [Elements of Fiction — Florida State College at Jacksonville](https://fscj.pressbooks.pub/literature/chapter/elements-of-fiction/) — links unreliable narration to credibility and suspense; used to require explicit authorization rather than accidental unreliability.
12. [Narration — Write What Matters](https://clc.pressbooks.pub/write/chapter/narration/) — explains how narrative position shapes tone, scope, and plot; used for unit-level POV consequences.
