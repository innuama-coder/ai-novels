# HI1 Methodology

## Model four distinct layers

Separate narrator voice (who speaks), focalizer (who perceives), character knowledge (what each actor may know), and reader knowledge (what the text has licensed the reader to infer). A grammatical person choice never grants omniscience. Treat every transfer of information as a state transition with a source, access path, time, and confidence.

## Build a knowledge ledger

For each narrative unit, record `known`, `unknown`, `misbelief`, `suspected`, and `withheld-from-reader` states for relevant characters and the reader. A later action is legal only if its required knowledge is present before that action. Dramatic irony is an intentional reader-ahead relation; mystery is an intentional reader-behind relation; suspense generally combines a known threat with uncertainty about outcome. These are designed relations, not vague moods.

## Design suspense as a closed obligation chain

Use `setup -> probe -> reveal -> payoff`:

- setup makes a question, object, anomaly, promise, or danger legitimately available;
- probe changes plausibility or stakes without resolving it;
- reveal changes a knowledge state and is supported by earlier evidence;
- payoff changes action, relationship, cost, or interpretation because of the reveal.

An authorized open terminal may replace reveal/payoff only when the source explicitly preserves the question. Fair misdirection offers evidence that supports a wrong but reasonable model while remaining retrospectively compatible with truth.

## Select POV by information function

Compare at least two legal POV allocations when freedom exists. Evaluate each by access to the required event, emotional stake, concealment cost, risk of unnatural ignorance, switch overhead, and downstream scene freedom. Choose the smallest POV system that can carry every information obligation without leakage. Mark prohibited POVs when their normal knowledge would collapse a suspense chain.

## Apply the honesty tests

Reject an architecture when it relies on:

- a POV character not noticing or thinking about decisive knowledge they naturally possess;
- a reveal introduced without prior availability;
- a red herring that is false only because the narrator lies outside an authorized unreliable-narrator contract;
- a character acting on information received later;
- a payoff that merely repeats the reveal and changes nothing;
- access to inner decisions that the author contract says must remain observable only through process.

## Preserve HLD granularity

Define what information must exist, who can hold it, the earliest/latest legal units, and its structural effect. Leave exact clue form, wording, gestures, memory fragments, dialogue, and paragraph-level placement to LLD and writing.
