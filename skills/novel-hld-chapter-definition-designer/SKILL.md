# Novel HLD Chapter Definition Designer

Turn each accepted chapter shell into one writing-ready chapter contract. HS1 continues to own storyline assignment; HD1 owns what the chapter must accomplish and what state it must leave behind.

## Required reading

Read [input-output-contract.md](references/input-output-contract.md), [chapter-definition-standard.md](references/chapter-definition-standard.md), [methodology.md](references/methodology.md), and [semantic-rubric.md](references/semantic-rubric.md) completely before execution.

## Workflow

1. Accept only a sealed HD1 work order with exact current HB0, HA1, HS1, HC1, HI1, HR1, and HK1 packages.
2. Inventory every HA1 chapter shell and its HS1 storyline placement. Define each exactly once and preserve chapter number, parent unit, title, and storyline roles.
3. For each chapter, bind the exact HI1 chapter POV assignment and its HC1 agency contract, then separately select named and/or institutional decision maker(s) and cost bearer(s) from the roles HC1 authorizes. Institutional actors must be copied exactly with their sources, responsibility, and authority limit; never assign their action to a convenient named character. Establish entry state, narrative necessity, knowledge boundary, immediate goal, active opposition, escalating pressure, consequential choice, cost, consequence, information change, relationship change, exit state, and chapter-end pull without assuming the focal character made every decision.
4. Bind required characters, environments, events, and key-scene functions. Do not invent an upstream fact or silently repair another stage.
5. If a chapter uses memory, apply the full nonlinear-memory contract: trigger, fragment scope, historical position, presentation position, initial interpretation, contradiction, present consequence, later verification or reinterpretation, truth status, and the explicit rule that memory is not objective evidence.
6. Define the LLD handoff: what scene design must solve, what it may choose freely, what it must not change, and how the completed detailed design will be accepted.
7. Validate the entire chapter chain. Each chapter must create a material change and make the next chapter necessary; no chapter may be filler, background-only, or a duplicate of another chapter's job.
8. Run `python scripts/run_skill.py <work-order.json> <draft-directory>` and let the shared Harness runtime seal the result.

## Hard boundaries

- Do not write scene beats, action order, dialogue, prose, imagery, sensory detail, props, or exact memory text.
- Do not change the number or order of HA1 chapter shells.
- Do not reassign HS1 storyline roles, repair character arcs, change information timing, or alter rhythm and continuity.
- Do not use generic statements such as “advance the plot”, “deepen the character”, or “add suspense” without a concrete state change and acceptance test.
- Do not treat a memory, simulation, dream, testimony, or model output as objective evidence unless upstream authority explicitly establishes that status.
- Do not resolve author-owned questions or hide missing HLD decisions inside LLD freedom.

## Done

Ready means every chapter shell has one complete definition; every POV exactly binds HI1; every consequential choice identifies its actual named or institutional actor(s) separately from its cost bearer(s); the chapter chain is continuous; every required HLD reference is placed; memory chapters pass the nonlinear-memory standard; every chapter has a meaningful change and next-chapter pull; and LLD can design scenes without inventing missing chapter-level structure.
