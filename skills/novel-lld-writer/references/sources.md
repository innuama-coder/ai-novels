# Novel LLD Method References

This file records the internet/research-backed methods used to harden `novel-lld-writer`. The skill remains bounded to LLD work: detailed settings and scene blueprints that inherit HLD, not PRD direction-setting, HLD architecture, or final prose.

## Source Map

| Source | LLD Use | Boundary Guard |
|---|---|---|
| Story Grid: Scene elements and value shift | Key scene blueprints should identify inciting pressure, complications, turning point, crisis/choice, climax/action, resolution/exit state, and value shift. | Use as a blueprint checklist, not as final prose. |
| Dwight V. Swain: Scene and Sequel | Distinguish active pressure (`goal/conflict/disaster`) from reactive processing (`reaction/dilemma/decision`) when detailing scene beats. | Do not rebuild HLD plot structure; only detail approved scene functions. |
| Reedsy: character profile and character development practices | Character details should include behavior, voice cues, desire/fear, contradictions, history pressure, and situation-specific reactions. | Do not invent new character arcs beyond HLD. |
| Reedsy: worldbuilding and setting practices | Space detail should include sensory state, rules, objects, social signals, movement constraints, and how the space shapes behavior. | Do not expand worldbuilding beyond HLD-defined spaces and functions. |
| Ursula K. Le Guin / craft guidance on viewpoint and voice | POV and voice cues should be operationalized as constraints for drafting: distance, diction, attention bias, and sensory filtering. | Do not rewrite HLD POV architecture. |
| Scene-level revision checklists from craft practice | Each scene blueprint should include who wants what, what blocks them, what changes, what information moves, and what must carry forward. | Do not write the scene itself. |
| Chekhov's gun / setup-payoff craft principle | Reusable objects, sensory cues, and callbacks should be registered with first-use and payoff intent. | Do not add new thematic promises outside PRD/HLD. |
| Save the Cat beat discipline | Scene blueprints should respect HLD beat intent and avoid flattening all scenes into the same emotional move. | Do not create a new beat sheet. |
| K.M. Weiland character arc practice | Character markers should show pressure on belief, desire, lie/truth, and decision behavior where HLD specifies arcs. | Do not redesign the arc. |
| Promise/progress/payoff craft principle | Route-node materials should maintain continuity by recording promises made, progress shown, and payoff hooks. | Do not redefine the route. |

## LLD Translation Rules

- Character detail: convert HLD role/arc into observable behavior, language, attention bias, decision tendency, and scenario triggers.
- Space detail: convert HLD space function into sensory state, physical constraints, object state, atmosphere, and role-space interaction.
- Route-node materialization: convert HLD route nodes into visible evidence, emotional pressure, information movement, and callback demands.
- Key scene blueprint: convert HLD scene function into time/place, value shift, pressure, turning point, decision, exit state, and drafting constraints.
- Writing handoff: package what to show, preserve, avoid, and carry forward.

## Forbidden Method Drift

- Do not use product PRD methods such as market sizing, competitive positioning, or business metrics.
- Do not use HLD methods to redesign route architecture, role arcs, theme, POV system, or key scene functions.
- Do not use writing methods to produce final prose, full dialogue, chapter drafts, or polished narrative paragraphs.
