# Research Sources

Accessed 2026-08-06. Method sources do not override the frozen HLD contract.

1. [Time — University of Tübingen](https://uni-tuebingen.de/en/233933) — distinguishes story/fabula from discourse and analyzes order, duration, and frequency; basis of the three-clock model.
2. [Rhythm — Réseau des narratologues francophone](https://wp.unil.ch/narratologie/2021/01/rythme-rythm/) — explains ellipse, summary, scene, and pause; used for duration-density bands.
3. [Narratological Time in Two African Novels — University of Education, Winneba](https://ir.uew.edu.gh/bitstream/handle/123456789/2221/Narratological%20time%20in%20Chinua%20Achebe%E2%80%99s%20Things%20Fall%20Apart%20And%20Ngugi%20Wa%20Thiong%E2%80%99o%E2%80%99s%20Weep%20Not%2C%20Child.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1) — applies order/duration/frequency and anachrony; used for reconstruction checks.
4. [Go Back in Time: Generating Flashbacks with Event Temporal Prompts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01898) — identifies event-order knowledge as essential to coherent flashbacks; used for entry/return and causal validation.
5. [Rhetoric and Rhythm — Open University](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/creative-writing?trackno=17) — treats rhythm as a reader relationship and structural effect; used to keep rhythm function-led.
6. [Master Narrative Pacing — MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-master-narrative-pacing) — recommends variation, breathers, order changes, and selective information; used for pacing option dimensions.
7. [David Baldacci on Pacing, Tension, and Suspense](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/david-baldaccis-writing-tips-for-pacing-tension-and-suspense) — emphasizes pace variation, anticipation, multiple viewpoints, and clocks; used for tension-pressure design.
8. [Pacing for Emotional Impact — Writer's Digest](https://www.writersdigest.com/pacing-for-emotional-impact-in-fiction-building-tension-and-release-in-a-novel) — frames pace as alternating tension and release; used for recovery-window criteria.
9. [How to Balance Action, Narrative, and Dialogue — Writer's Digest](https://www.writersdigest.com/write-better-fiction/how-to-balance-action-narrative-and-dialogue-in-your-novel) — connects mode balance to pace; used only at HLD density level, not prose prescription.
10. [Need to Kick It Up a Notch? — Writer's Digest](https://www.writersdigest.com/writing-articles/need-to-kick-it-up-a-notch) — distinguishes pace modes and reader effects; used to reject uniform pacing.
11. [Tighten the Tension in Your Novel — Writer's Digest](https://www.writersdigest.com/there-are-no-rules/tighten-the-tension-in-your-novel) — stresses planted information and post-high tension management; used for continuity with HI1 reveals.
12. [Scene Structure and Transitions in Big Scenes — Helping Writers Become Authors](https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/scene-structure-and-transitions-in-big-scenes/) — relates action/reaction weighting to pace; used to require functional recovery rather than dead air.
