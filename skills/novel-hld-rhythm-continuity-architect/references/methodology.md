# HR1 Methodology

## Keep three clocks separate

Model (1) story time: when events occur in-world, (2) discourse order: when narrative units present them, and (3) character-perceived time: how duration/sequence is experienced. Genette's order, duration, and frequency provide the base model. Do not treat chapter order as event chronology.

## Treat duration as a ratio, not a word target

Classify each unit's treatment as pause, scene-like expansion, summary/compression, or ellipse, then record the narrative function and relative density. A high-stakes moment may be expanded; transit or repetition may be compressed. Exact paragraphs and word counts belong downstream.

## Build a multidimensional continuity ledger

Track time, location, knowledge, resource, permission, physical state, relationship state, and unresolved obligation. Each transition requires a source and a persistence/release rule. A later unit cannot consume a resource, authority, relationship state, or piece of knowledge that the ledger has not made available.

## Validate space through affordances

Use only existing environment/world facts. For every cross-location transition, test distance, available mechanism, travel/communication time, access authority, resource cost, and synchronization with parallel tracks. If the source lacks a fact required to make the transition possible, block or return; do not invent a tunnel, device, clearance, or shortcut.

## Design rhythm as contrast with purpose

Pace is reader-experienced progression, not merely action frequency. Compare at least two legal arrangements of expansion/compression, storyline switching, and information density. Give recovery windows a job: processing consequence, relationship shift, reorientation, or preparation. Recovery lowers immediate intensity while preserving the underlying dramatic question.

## Design tension as state change

For each tension segment record pressure source, stakes, uncertainty, available choices, tension-in, turn, tension-out, and carryover. Avoid a straight rising line: contrast and partial release make later escalation legible. Climax requires prior accumulation and a consequential choice/result; resolution requires enough space for consequence without dissolving future obligations.

## Preserve HLD granularity

Define unit-level ordering, relative density, state transitions, synchronization and continuity constraints. Leave exact scene count, moment-to-moment schedule, gestures, beats, transitions, prose rhythm, and paragraph length to LLD/writing.
