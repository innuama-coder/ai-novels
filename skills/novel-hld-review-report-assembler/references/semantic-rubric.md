# Semantic rubric

Scripts do not decide story quality or semantic fidelity. A reviewer records pass/fail evidence for each item.

## Pass anchors

- The first screen/page-equivalent states the title, HLD scope, current status, and exact author decision.
- Each of the nine HLD domains states conclusions before support and makes its control logic understandable in ordinary language.
- Every sealed conception character and environment has one complete, independently readable definition card; names, functions, limits and dramatic use are not collapsed into arc summaries or setting lists.
- Every HP1 material control is locatable; no control is omitted to satisfy the budget.
- Must/must-not/open/LLD-freedom strength is unchanged.
- No sentence or visual data adds a story fact or “helpfully” repairs HP1.
- Visuals model real multi-part relationships and remain understandable through text.
- Open items and LLD boundary are explicit enough that approval does not imply approval of undeveloped detail.
- The report contains no candidate comparison, reasoning chain, production diary, or self-congratulation.
- The author can answer: what is the whole architecture, how lines connect, where arcs turn, how information is controlled, how rhythm escalates, what key scenes must accomplish, and what remains open/deferred.

## Revise anchors

Return locally when meaning is intact but conclusions are buried, sections are misordered, visuals are redundant, bindings are malformed, questions are vague, author-facing prose sounds like a machine or process memo, or the budget exceeds eighteen minutes.

## Block anchors

Return upstream only when HP1 itself lacks a necessary conclusion or has irresolvable internal absence. Do not use upstream return for a report-writing defect.

## Six-dimension gate

Each case must score 2/2 for scope fidelity, input-gate obedience, output-contract correctness, semantic/deterministic correctness, boundary discipline, and parent consumability. Any hard-boundary breach is an automatic fail.
