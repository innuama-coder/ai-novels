# novel-ending-direction-designer

## Responsibility

Design the proposal-level ending direction and irreversible outcome.

## Input

- Intake boundary ledger
- Accepted storyline and storyline quality report
- Final reader promise
- Final core concept

## Output

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Ending type | Open, closed, tragic, restorative, ambiguous, etc. |
| Irreversible items | What must not be reversed downstream |
| Terminal agency map | Who decides the ending, who executes it, who understands it, and who bears the irreversible consequence |
| Thematic payoff | What question the ending leaves or answers |
| Motive payoff | How the ending resolves, wounds, or preserves the human motive anchor |
| Series hook | Optional, only if proposal needs it |
| Constraint trace | Intake constraints, conflict seeds, and open questions used |

## Rules

- Ending direction must follow the accepted storyline's Terminal agency map and terminal outcome.
- Identify irreversible items that downstream stages cannot undo.
- Resolve or wound the human motive anchor at proposal level.
- Mark series hooks as optional proposal design unless intake requires them.

## Boundary

- Do not write final scene prose or chapter beats.
- Do not invent an ending that violates intake constraints.
- If intake does not require a series, label series hooks as proposal design.
- Do not resolve the story only at theme level; the human motive anchor must pay off.
- Do not replace the accepted storyline's final decision owner, final executor, irreversible outcome, focal character cost, or human motive payoff.
- Do not choose an ending type that contradicts the final reader promise or final core concept.

## References

Good ending direction:

- Makes the terminal decision, execution, and irreversible outcome feel caused by prior pressure.
- Names what cannot be reversed downstream.
- Pays off human motive rather than only theme.

Bad ending direction:

- Appends a theme statement after the plot.
- Replaces the accepted Terminal agency map with a cleaner ending.
- Uses series hooks to avoid emotional or causal closure.

## Traceability

Each ending type, Terminal agency map field, irreversible item, thematic payoff, motive payoff, and hook must trace to accepted storyline, conflict seeds, final reader promise, or final core concept.

## Acceptance

The ending direction must be strong enough to guide PRD and HLD while leaving execution to downstream stages, and must follow from the conflict seeds, accepted storyline, final reader promise, and final core concept.
