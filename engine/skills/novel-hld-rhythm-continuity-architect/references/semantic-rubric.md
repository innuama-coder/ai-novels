# HR1 Semantic Rubric

## Pass/revise/fail

- `pass`: all anchors pass with object-level evidence and no actionable finding.
- `revise`: only HR1 ordering, density, switching, synchronization, pacing, tension, recovery, or ledger projection needs local correction.
- `fail`: an upstream event time, environment capability, world mechanism, story-line obligation, character state, information rule, author answer, or reality fact must change.

## Pass anchors

1. Chronology is reconstructable and causal prerequisites precede effects.
2. Discourse reorderings have explicit triggers/returns and preserve current-state obligations.
3. Space/communication transitions are possible under known distance, mechanism, duration, resource, and permission constraints.
4. Parallel tracks synchronize at every crossing and convergence.
5. Every tracked state has a legal predecessor, change source, persistence interval, and release condition.
6. Pace and density vary for a stated narrative function rather than a template.
7. Tension has accumulation, reversals, partial releases, recovery, and climax preparation; it is not monotonic noise.
8. POV and knowledge legality survive the chosen order.
9. Authority and open items remain unchanged; no LLD/prose leakage occurs.
10. Package/result/completion separation lets the parent make one deterministic next decision.

## Blockers

Time inversion that breaks causality; impossible movement/communication; state teleport; unresolved parallel mismatch; reveal before legal acquisition; monotone tension; missing climax accumulation; invented setting affordance; exact scene schedule or prose instruction; `required_now` with candidate; unauthorized read; ownership conflation; ambiguous parent result.

## Counterexample anchor

A character exits a secure base and appears in a remote city in the next concurrent unit, although the world model supplies neither travel time nor authorized transport. A chapter break does not repair the continuity defect; HR1 must not pass or invent transport.
