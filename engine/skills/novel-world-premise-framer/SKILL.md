# novel-world-premise-framer

## Responsibility

Define only the world premise needed for the proposal to make sense.

## Input

- Intake boundary ledger
- Accepted storyline and storyline quality report
- Final reader promise
- Final core concept

## Output

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| World premise | Minimal setting and premise assumptions |
| Necessary rules | Only rules needed to understand the concept |
| Pressure function | How the world premise intensifies the intake conflict seeds |
| Unknowns | World questions to leave for PRD/HLD |
| Constraint trace | Intake constraints, conflict seeds, and open questions used |

## Rules

- Define only world assumptions needed to understand the proposal.
- Make each rule serve story pressure, not replace human motive.
- Leave system mechanics and full world structure to PRD/HLD unless needed for proposal clarity.
- Preserve accepted storyline, final reader promise, and final core concept.

## Boundary

- Do not write a world bible.
- Do not design institutions, technology, geography, history, or social systems beyond proposal need.
- Do not import old HLD/LLD world details as frozen.
- Do not let world rules replace human motive as the cause of core conflict.
- Do not add world rules that change the accepted storyline's causal chain, Terminal agency map, human motive anchor, or terminal outcome.
- Do not add world rules that weaken the final reader promise or turn the final core concept into a generic setting premise.

## References

Good world premise:

- Makes the story possible and pressurized with minimal assumptions.
- Names unknowns that PRD/HLD must later design.
- Keeps human motive, not world mechanics, at the center of decisive conflict.

Bad world premise:

- Expands into institution, technology, history, or geography bible.
- Solves plot problems by adding rules that rewrite the accepted storyline.
- Makes the proposal read like setting exposition instead of story.

## Traceability

Each world premise, necessary rule, pressure function, and unknown must trace to intake constraints, accepted storyline needs, final reader promise, or final core concept.

## Acceptance

The world premise is acceptable if it supports the final reader promise, final core concept, and accepted storyline pressure without closing HLD design space.
