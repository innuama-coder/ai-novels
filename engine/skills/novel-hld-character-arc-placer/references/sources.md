# HC1 research sources

Accessed: 2026-08-06. Sources inform the HC1 reasoning model but never grant
authority to invent character facts.

| # | Source | URL | Absorbed point |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Open University, *Start writing fiction* printable course | https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/creative-writing/start-writing-fiction/altformat-printable | Character plus conflict produces plot; contradictions and conflict reveal complex character. |
| 2 | Open University, *Start writing fiction: characters and stories* | https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/start-writing-fiction-characters-and-stories | Character and plot develop together; conflict and portrayal should be tested through events rather than profile accumulation. |
| 3 | MasterClass, *How to Develop a Fictional Character* | https://www.masterclass.com/articles/writing-tips-for-character-development | Character development joins clear motivation with changes caused by actions and experience; plot and character are inseparable. |
| 4 | MasterClass, *How to Write a Captivating Character Arc* | https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-write-a-captivating-character-arc | Transformational, positive, negative, and flat arcs are all possible; role and larger plot constrain arc shape. |
| 5 | MasterClass, *Character Development Definition* | https://www.masterclass.com/articles/character-development-definition | Begin dramatic characterization from intention, obstacle, and tactics; not every character needs an equally large arc. |
| 6 | MasterClass, *Learn About Narrative Arcs* | https://www.masterclass.com/articles/what-are-the-elements-of-a-narrative-arc-and-how-do-you-create-one-in-writing | Story arc is external while character arc tracks internal change; climax commonly forces a consequential choice. |
| 7 | K. M. Weiland, *How to Use a Truth Chart to Figure Out Your Character's Arc* | https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/figure-out-your-characters-arc/ | Want, need, belief, truth, and formative causes can organize an arc, but must become story-specific pressures and choices. |
| 8 | K. M. Weiland, *5 Ways to Use Theme to Create Character Arc* | https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/use-theme-to-create-character-arc/ | Inner conflict must cause and be caused by outer conflict; meaningful thematic choice must carry a real cost. |
| 9 | K. M. Weiland, *How to Write a Negative Character Arc, Part 1* | https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/negative-character-arc-1/ | Negative arcs use the same wants, needs, and pressures but trace rejection or corruption and resulting loss. |
| 10 | Jane Friedman, *Building a Believable Chain of Events in Your Novel* | https://janefriedman.com/believable-events/ | Stories move choice to consequence and stimulus to response; setting, context, pursuit, and characterization must jointly justify action. |
| 11 | Reedsy, *What Is the Narrative Arc?* | https://reedsy.com/blog/narrative-arc/ | Plot points should move characters nearer to or farther from goals; internal and external arcs are symbiotic but distinct. |
| 12 | Story Grid, *Worldview Story Cheat Sheet* | https://storygrid.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/WORLDVIEW-CHEAT-SHEET-Google-Docs.pdf | Worldview arcs can move through maturation, education, disillusionment, or revelation; value direction must be explicit. |
