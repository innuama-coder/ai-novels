# Methodology

## 1. Decision-first information architecture

Begin with the story the author is being asked to approve, then expose the minimum complete conclusion layer. Front-load the premise, central relationship, main pressure, and ending shape in natural prose. Use descriptive headings so a scanning reader can predict each section. “Short” means decision-proportionate, not incomplete.

## 2. Progressive disclosure without concealment

Layer information as: story overview → explicit character definitions → explicit environment definitions → the remaining HLD conclusions → useful relationships/visual models → genuine author-owned open items. Essential story controls stay in the primary path. Supporting locators, validation explanations, workflow status, and machine classifications remain machine-readable and never enter the author surface. Progressive disclosure is never permission to hide a material story control.

The storyline section includes one human chapter card per designed chapter.
Each card says, in this order: the chapter name, what the chapter must make
clear, which named storylines it principally or secondarily advances (including
background pressure or intentionally open lines), and what changes by the end.
Never show shell IDs, design IDs, placement vocabulary, or audit explanations.
Never turn these cards into scene lists or prewritten prose.

## 3. Evidence-bound compression

Treat HP1 as immutable evidence. Build a material-control inventory before drafting. A report sentence may combine controls only when its source binding retains every contributing source ID and locator and its modality is unchanged. Preserve `must`, `must not`, `open`, and `LLD freedom` distinctly.

## 4. Visuals as relationship models

Use a visual model only when it reduces the cost of understanding a multi-node relation, sequence, convergence, information path, or pacing pattern. A visual has a conclusion, textual role, review question, and source bindings. It is semantic data for HH1-C, never SVG markup. Avoid decorative or duplicate visuals.

## 5. Accessible dual encoding

Every visual model must be understandable through its textual conclusion and `visual_role`; do not rely on color, position, or shape alone. Section order remains meaningful without rendering. HH1-C later supplies accessible HTML/SVG semantics.

## 6. Twenty-four-minute budget as a lossless constraint

Count author-visible text deterministically and add 0.25 minutes per visual. If over twenty-four minutes, remove repetition, shorten headings/questions, merge semantically compatible elements, and keep machine trace outside the reader surface. Never delete a material fact or invent a summary that weakens it. The allowance exists for concrete character, place, city, season, ordinary-person, and chapter definitions, not process prose.

Author-visible prose follows ordinary human discourse: name the person or place, state what happens, and explain why the consequence matters. Do not expose pipeline vocabulary, audit vocabulary, field labels, responsibility chains, or compressed semicolon summaries. Exact machine structures may remain sealed for validation, but every city, route, resident group, character, and concrete place must have a separate natural-language projection for the author.

## 7. Separation of duties

HH1-A assembles. HH1-B independently proves fidelity and decision usability. HH1-C renders. The publisher seals all three. This prevents self-certification and keeps presentation changes from mutating the machine HLD.
