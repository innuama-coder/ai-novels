# HS1 research sources

| # | Source | URL | Absorbed point |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | MasterClass, *How to Structure a Story* | https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-structure-a-story | Parallel structures may unfold concurrently and intersect; all introduced plotlines must remain under deliberate control. |
| 2 | MasterClass, *How to Master the Art of Plot Development* | https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-master-the-art-of-plot-development | Subplots should deepen character, backstory, or theme and weave into the main action rather than distract from it. |
| 3 | Dan Brown, *Creating Suspense, Part 1* | https://www.masterclass.com/classes/dan-brown-teaches-writing-thrillers/chapters/creating-suspense-part-1 | Parallel plotlines can create suspense when their alignment and contrast are deliberate. |
| 4 | Writer's Digest, *Two Timing—Building Narrative Power by Telling Two Stories at Once* | https://www.writersdigest.com/two-timing-building-narrative-power-by-telling-two-stories-at-once | Multiple lines add layers and meaning when aligned; transitions need reliable reader orientation. |
| 5 | Writer's Digest, *How to Build Subplots From Multiple Viewpoints* | https://www.writersdigest.com/write-better-fiction/how-to-build-subplots-from-multiple-viewpoints | Independently introduced lines need development and a credible merge; uncontrolled minor lines can displace the main plot. |
| 6 | The Novelry, *Weaving the Plot: How to Write a Brilliant Subplot* | https://www.thenovelry.com/blog/how-to-write-a-subplot | A subplot is a secondary but purposeful line that can deepen theme, genre, backstory, or a later reveal. |
| 7 | Jane Friedman, *Does Your Multiple Storyline Novel Work?* | https://janefriedman.com/making-multiple-storylines-work-in-your-novel/ | Each line must be necessary, fully developed, and connected by impact, illumination, reflection, or a common whole. |
| 8 | Jane Friedman, *How to Skillfully Use Subplots in Your Novel* | https://janefriedman.com/how-to-use-subplots/ | Treat subplots as interwoven strands; different strands may dominate at different stages and collide at turns. |
| 9 | Jane Friedman, *Using Multiple Points of View* | https://janefriedman.com/using-multiple-points-view/ | A line or viewpoint needs its own story purpose; extra characters and plots should arise organically from the central problem. |
| 10 | K. M. Weiland, *Writing Multiple Plotlines: Everything You Need to Know* | https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/writing-multiple-plotlines-everything-you-need-to-know/amp/ | Concurrent plotlines need full structure and thematic resonance; a removable subplot likely is not integral. |
| 11 | K. M. Weiland, *5 Tips for Organizing Subplots* | https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/5-tips-organizing-subplots/ | Strong subplots become inseparable from the larger plot instead of forming an unrelated add-on. |
| 12 | K. M. Weiland, *How to Structure Stories With Multiple Main Characters* | https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/how-to-structure-stories-with-multiple-main-characters/amp/ | Multiple protagonists require a clear relationship between each external plot, internal movement, and the global structure. |
